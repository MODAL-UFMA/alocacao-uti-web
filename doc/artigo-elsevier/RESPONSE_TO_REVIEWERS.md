## Response to Major Comments

### P1. Clinical Calibration: Measured vs. Calibrated vs. Assumed Parameters & Admitted Mortality Verification
> *The clinical calibration is presented as more empirical than it is. Section 4.1 says the model avoids “arbitrary synthetic parameters”, and the highlights say “calibrated with CNES and Epimed data”. However: $P_{\text{out}}$ and $g$ are counterfactual; for the adult group, $\mathbb{E}[P_{\text{in}}] \approx 0.66$ (34% mortality) while the text cites “near 27%”; $W_{\max} \sim U(0.5, 2.5)$ does not come from Cardoso et al. [12], and it is not explained how $\alpha = 0.18\text{/day}$ was derived; [16] (Guinsburg et al.) only covers VLBW infants whose stay is much longer than 11.5 days; ORCHESTRA and “AMIB pediatric data” are cited without reference. The authors must separate, in a table, what is measured, what is fitted to reproduce a measure, and what is assumed, and show that the admitted mortality generated by the model matches the registries.*

**Response:**  
We completely agree and have overhauled Section 4.1, the Abstract, the Highlights, and **Table 2** (`\ref{tab:calibration_params}`) to remove overclaims and establish a strict three-category epistemological taxonomy:
1. **(I) Directly Measured (Registry Data):** Facility bed capacities $C_j$ ($15$ Adult, $10$ Coronary, $10$ Pediatric, $20$ Neonatal; total $55$ beds) from DATASUS/CNES (`2111302726653`), and specialty-level mean LOS ($\mu_{\text{los}, j}$) and hospital mortality targets from the AMIB/Epimed *UTIs Brasileiras* registry \cite{utisbrasileiras2024} and the ORCHESTRA studies \cite{soares2024orchestra, soares2024update, zampieri2019icu}.
2. **(II) Calibrated / Derived to Match Registry Targets:** We recalibrated the uniform ranges of $P_{\text{out}}^i$ and $g_i$ so that the theoretical and simulation-generated post-admission mortality rates $M_{\text{adm}, j}^{\pi} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_j^\pi|}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_j^\pi} (1 - P_{\text{in}}^i(w_i))$ (Eq. 15) match the AMIB/Epimed registry targets across all four pools:
   - **Adult ICU:** $P_{\text{out}} \sim U(0.15, 0.55)$, $g \sim U(0.45, 0.75) \implies 1 - \mathbb{E}[P_{\text{in},0}] = (1-0.35)(1-0.60) = \mathbf{26.0\%}$. In the simulation, admitted adult mortality is **$25.3\%$–$26.1\%$** at $\rho=1.0$ and **$25.5\%$–$30.2\%$** at $\rho=1.6$ (matching the AMIB/Epimed public SUS target of $\approx 26\%$–$28\%$).
   - **Coronary (UCO):** $P_{\text{out}} \sim U(0.30, 0.70)$, $g \sim U(0.55, 0.85) \implies 1 - \mathbb{E}[P_{\text{in},0}] = \mathbf{15.0\%}$. Simulated admitted mortality: **$15.2\%$–$15.8\%$** ($\rho=1.0$) and **$16.3\%$–$19.1\%$** ($\rho=1.6$).
   - **Pediatric ICU:** $P_{\text{out}} \sim U(0.50, 0.90)$, $g \sim U(0.65, 0.90) \implies 1 - \mathbb{E}[P_{\text{in},0}] = \mathbf{6.75\%}$. Simulated admitted mortality: **$6.8\%$–$7.2\%$** ($\rho=1.0$) and **$8.0\%$–$9.5\%$** ($\rho=1.6$).
   - **Neonatal ICU:** $P_{\text{out}} \sim U(0.45, 0.85)$, $g \sim U(0.60, 0.88) \implies 1 - \mathbb{E}[P_{\text{in},0}] = \mathbf{9.1\%}$. Simulated admitted mortality: **$8.6\%$–$9.0\%$** ($\rho=1.0$) and **$10.0\%$–$11.8\%$** ($\rho=1.6$).
   - **Derivation of $\alpha_{\text{det}}$:** Section 4.1 now explicitly explains that Cardoso et al. \cite{cardoso2011impact} report an additional mortality hazard of $\approx 1.5\%$ per hour of pre-ICU boarding delay ($\approx 0.36\text{ day}^{-1}$ on total mortality), which corresponds to an exponential decay rate of $\alpha_{\text{det}} \approx 0.18\text{ day}^{-1}$ on the saveable survival margin $P_{\text{in},0}^i - P_{\text{out}}^i$ (so that a 48-hour boarding delay erodes $1 - e^{-0.36} \approx 30\%$ of the initial ICU benefit).
3. **(III) Assumed (Counterfactual / Sensitivity):** The counterfactual ward survival $P_{\text{out}}^i$, fractional efficacy $g_i$, waiting tolerance $W_{\max}^i \sim U(0.5, 2.5)$ days, severity--LOS coupling $\beta$, and reneging payoff factor $\theta_{\text{ren}}$ are explicitly labeled as modeling assumptions and subjected to wide sensitivity analyses.
4. **Neonatal LOS and Missing Citations:** We clarified in Section 4.1 that $\mu_{\text{los, neonatal}} = 11.5$ days represents the overall Type II Neonatal ICU average across all neonatal admissions (blending general term/moderate preterm neonates staying 5–8 days with the VLBW preterm subgroup studied in Guinsburg et al. \cite{guinsburg2016death}, whose stays exceed 30–45 days), and we added formal bibliographic entries for the ORCHESTRA study (`soares2024orchestra`, DOI `10.62675/2965-2774.20240118-en`) and the AMIB/Epimed *UTIs Brasileiras* registry (`utisbrasileiras2024`).

### P2. Validity and Tightness of the Offline LP Upper Bound
> *(a) Constraint (19) includes inherited occupancy from the warm-up. If this comes from a specific policy, the bound is invalid for others. State which initial state was used, or include the warm-up. (b) State whether adult and coronary form a single group $g$ with $C = 25$, and whether $S_{\max}$ covers drainage. (c) Replace “LP/MILP” with “LP bound”. Furthermore, because the bound knows realized LOS (556 admissions vs. 459), the 9.9% gap is dominated by clairvoyance. Provide a tighter bound—e.g., an information relaxation (Brown, Smith & Sun, 2010) or the same LP using $\mathbb{E}[\text{LOS}_i]$—and present the cherry-picking caveat alongside the gap in the Abstract and text.*

**Response:**  
We have fully resolved (a), (b), and (c) in Section 3.4 (`\ref{sec:milp_bound}`) and Section 5.1, and implemented the tighter **Duration-Expectation LP Bound ($\mathbb{E}[\text{LOS}_i]$)** alongside the **Hindsight LP Bound (realized $\text{LOS}_i$)**:
- **(a) Policy-Independent Initial State:** The LP is solved strictly over the evaluated cohort $\mathcal{I}_{\text{eval}} = \{i : T_{\text{warm}} \le t_{\text{arr}}^i < T_{\text{warm}} + T\}$ starting from **empty beds at $t = T_{\text{warm}}$** (zero legacy occupancy from $[0, T_{\text{warm}})$). Because every online policy $\pi$ faces non-negative bed occupancy inherited from the warm-up period on $[T_{\text{warm}}, T_{\text{warm}} + T)$, dropping warm-up occupancy strictly enlarges the capacity region for $\mathcal{I}_{\text{eval}}$, making the LP relaxation **100% policy-independent and valid for all policies**.
- **(b) Pooled Adult+Coronary Capacity ($C=25$) and Full Drainage ($S_{\max}$):** Section 3.4 explicitly states that when overflow is active, the LP combines Adult and Coronary beds into a single pooled group $g_{\text{adult+coronary}}$ with $C_{\text{adult+coronary}} = 15 + 10 = 25$ beds (a valid relaxation of hierarchical sharing), and dynamically sets $S_{\max} = \max_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{eval}}} \{s_{\text{arr}}^i + K_i + L_i\} + 2$ so that $100\%$ of post-horizon queue drainage and service completion is covered.
- **(c) Terminology & Tightened Duration-Expectation LP Bound ($\mathbb{E}[\text{LOS}_i]$):** We replaced “LP/MILP” with “LP Bound” throughout the paper, cited Brown, Smith & Sun (2010, *Operations Research*, DOI `10.1287/opre.1090.0796`) \cite{brown2010information}, and solved both LP formulations across all 400 replications (reported in **Table 3** and **Figure 1**):
  - Under the **Hindsight LP Bound ($S_{\text{UB}}^{\text{real}}$, using realized $\text{LOS}_i$)** at $\rho = 1.60$, the offline solver cherry-picks ultra-short log-normal realizations, admitting $614.4$ patients (vs. $454.2$ for CMU-MGS) and yielding $590.47$ survivors (a pessimistic gap of **$9.89\%$**).
  - Under the **Duration-Expectation LP Bound ($S_{\text{UB}}^{\text{exp}}$, using $\mathbb{E}[\text{LOS}_i]$)** at $\rho = 1.60$, offline admissions drop to $495.7$ and the bound tightens to $559.17$ survivors, cutting the optimality gap of CMU-MGS by more than half—from **$9.89\%$ down to $4.84\%$** ($0.42\%$ at $\rho=0.70$, $2.44\%$ at $\rho=1.00$, $4.32\%$ at $\rho=1.30$, and $4.84\%$ at $\rho=1.60$). Both gaps and the cherry-picking explanation now appear together in the Abstract, Table 3, Figure 1, and Section 5.1.

### P3. Survival Payoff of Reneging Patients ($\theta_{\text{ren}} \in \{1.0, 0.5, 0.0\}$)
> *$W_{\max}$ is defined as the deadline before “pre-ICU death, emergency transfer, or loss of therapeutic window”. Yet every reneging patient receives $P_{\text{out}}$ (ward survival). If part of reneging is death, the payoff should be 0 (or a mixture). This favors policies with more reneging (MSF, MGS). At least one sensitivity scenario with a lower payoff for reneging patients is required.*

**Response:**  
We parameterized the survival payoff of reneging patients as $\theta_{\text{ren}} P_{\text{out}}^i$ with $\theta_{\text{ren}} \in \{1.0, 0.5, 0.0\}$ in Section 3.1.1 (Eqs. 9–14) and ran a dedicated 100-replication CRN experiment at $\rho = 1.60$ across all six policies, reported in the new **Section 5.4.3** and **Table 6** (`\ref{tab:reneging_payoff}`):
- When $\theta_{\text{ren}} = 0.5$ (each reneging patient receives $0.5 \times P_{\text{out}}^i$, representing a 50/50 mixture of ward survival and pre-ICU death):
  - **MSF collapses:** Because MSF has the highest reneging rate ($40.2\%$), its gain over FCFS drops from $+14.12$ lives ($\theta_{\text{ren}}=1.0$) to just $+1.45 \pm 0.60$ lives ($\theta_{\text{ren}}=0.5$), and turns negative ($-11.22 \pm 0.89$ lives) when $\theta_{\text{ren}} = 0.0$.
  - **LSF overtakes greedy MGS:** Because LSF has a lower reneging rate ($36.6\%$ vs. $38.6\%$), LSF rises to $463.35$ survivors at $\theta_{\text{ren}} = 0.5$, surpassing greedy MGS ($458.01$ survivors).
  - **CMU-MGS remains Rank 1 and widens its lead over MGS:** Because CMU-MGS normalizes net survival gain $P_{\text{in}}^i(w) - \theta_{\text{ren}} P_{\text{out}}^i$ by $\mathbb{E}[\text{LOS}_i]$, it automatically accelerates bed turnover as $\theta_{\text{ren}}$ falls (admitting $475.5$ patients at $\theta_{\text{ren}}=0.5$ and $478.9$ at $\theta_{\text{ren}}=0.0$). Its paired advantage over greedy MGS expands more than threefold from **$+1.48 \pm 0.26$ lives** ($\theta_{\text{ren}}=1.0$) to **$+5.41 \pm 0.50$ lives** ($\theta_{\text{ren}}=0.5$, $t = 10.77, p_{\text{Holm}} < 0.001$) and **$+3.91 \pm 0.67$ lives** ($\theta_{\text{ren}}=0.0$, $p_{\text{Holm}} < 0.001$).

## Direct Answers to the Referee's Four Questions

1. **Which initial state (occupancy at the end of warm-up) is used in the LP for each policy?**  
   The LP uses an **empty bed state at $t = T_{\text{warm}}$** (zero legacy occupancy from warm-up arrivals) and optimizes strictly over the evaluated cohort $\mathcal{I}_{\text{eval}} = [T_{\text{warm}}, T_{\text{warm}}+T)$. Because every online policy $\pi$ faces non-negative legacy bed occupation at $T_{\text{warm}}$, relaxing legacy occupation to zero strictly enlarges the capacity region for $\mathcal{I}_{\text{eval}}$, yielding a **single, policy-independent upper bound** that is valid for every online policy $\pi$.

2. **What is the admitted mortality generated per group, compared with the registries?**  
   As reported in **Table 2** (`\ref{tab:calibration_params}`), the simulation-generated hospital mortality among admitted patients ($M_{\text{adm}, j}^\pi$) closely matches the AMIB/Epimed registry targets across all four groups:
   - **Adult ICU:** $25.3\%$ (CMU-MGS) to $26.1\%$ (FCFS) at $\rho=1.0$, and $25.5\%$ to $30.2\%$ at $\rho=1.6$ (Registry target: $\approx 26\%$–$28\%$).
   - **Coronary (UCO):** $15.2\%$ to $15.8\%$ at $\rho=1.0$, and $16.3\%$ to $19.1\%$ at $\rho=1.6$ (Registry target: $\approx 14\%$–$17\%$).
   - **Pediatric ICU:** $6.8\%$ to $7.2\%$ at $\rho=1.0$, and $8.0\%$ to $9.5\%$ at $\rho=1.6$ (Registry target: $\approx 5\%$–$8\%$).
   - **Neonatal ICU:** $8.6\%$ to $9.0\%$ at $\rho=1.0$, and $10.0\%$ to $11.8\%$ at $\rho=1.6$ (Registry target: $\approx 8\%$–$11\%$).

3. **How does the policy ordering change if reneging patients have survival $0.5 \times P_{\text{out}}$?**  
   As shown in **Table 6** (`\ref{tab:reneging_payoff}`) at $\rho = 1.60$:
   - Under baseline $\theta_{\text{ren}} = 1.0$: `CMU-MGS (532.09) > MGS (530.61) > AUSTERE (530.27) > MSF (527.54) > LSF (525.74) > FCFS (513.42)`.
   - Under $\theta_{\text{ren}} = 0.5$ ($0.5 \times P_{\text{out}}^i$): **`CMU-MGS (463.42) >= LSF (463.35) > AUSTERE (460.71) > MGS (458.01) > MSF (445.67) > FCFS (444.23)`**. Specifically, high-reneging policies (MSF and greedy MGS) are heavily penalized, LSF rises to second place due to its high admission volume, and **CMU-MGS remains Rank 1**, expanding its paired advantage over greedy MGS from $+1.48$ lives to **$+5.41 \pm 0.50$ lives** ($p_{\text{Holm}} < 0.001$).
   - Under $\theta_{\text{ren}} = 0.0$ (zero payoff): **`CMU-MGS (404.87) > AUSTERE (403.90) > MGS = LSF (400.96) > FCFS (375.03) > MSF (363.81)`**.

4. **By how much does the gap drop if the LP uses $\mathbb{E}[\text{LOS}_i]$ instead of realized LOS?**  
   At $\rho = 1.60$, replacing realized $\text{LOS}_i$ with $\mathbb{E}[\text{LOS}_i]$ in the LP reduces offline cherry-picked admissions from $614.4$ to $495.7$ and lowers the upper bound from $590.47$ to $559.17$ expected survivors. Consequently, the optimality gap of CMU-MGS **drops by more than half, from $9.89\%$ down to $4.84\%$** (and across all regimes $\rho \in \{0.7, 1.0, 1.3, 1.6\}$, from $\{0.51\%, 3.39\%, 7.25\%, 9.89\%\}$ down to $\{0.42\%, 2.44\%, 4.32\%, 4.84\%\}$).
